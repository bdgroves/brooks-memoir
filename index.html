The Baseball Gods of East Texas

Spring 1989. A California kid who could field but never really hit discovers that in a small Texas town, baseball isn't a sport — it's an identity. An immaculate infield beside cracked tennis courts, the crackle of the PA drifting across campus, the ting of a bat catching one perfectly, and the spring the whole dorm went to see Major League together. Turns out Blinn sent seventeen players to the majors. Not really about baseball at all — about how many different versions of America there are.

The Vikings of Brenham

A kid from California, five giant Swedish throwers — hammer, discus, shot put — and a Cadillac painted like the Berlin Wall — all somehow deposited in the middle of East Texas at once. Blinn ran one of the greatest juco track programs that ever existed, and it pulled the whole world into one little German-Texas town. The Swedes leaned all the way into being Swedish, and taught a nineteen-year-old that being exactly who you are is a wonderful thing. The one where all the other stories come together.

The Tape from Cal Poly

The summer of 1989, home in Groveland after that first year at Blinn — an empty condo at Pine Mountain Lake, sleeping bags on the floor, cheap beer, and a friend back from Cal Poly with a cassette tape and four words: you have to hear this. The album was Diesel and Dust by Midnight Oil, and thirty-seven years later he's still listening. A love letter to being nineteen and discovering the world is so much bigger than you imagined.

Across the Hall

A California kid and an East Texas forestry major who smelled like a health-food store lived directly across the hall in Memorial Hall — and could not have been more different. Vitamins by the tackle box, PTL tapes under the bed, soil that must never be called dirt, a blue Jeep, and the day Todd walked a first-time voter to the polls in 1988. He now lives in Ecuador raising fancy nuts. Nearly forty years on, he's still one of the strangest and nicest people I've ever known.

Cow Roping on a Saturday Morning

Deep in the Heart of Texas

The first in a new series. In the fall of 1988 an eighteen-year-old from the Sierra foothills — not homesick, exactly, just never quite sure where home was — gets shipped off to the one college that accepted him — Blinn, the oldest junior college in Texas, in the middle of German farm country. He arrives in a giant Cadillac driven by the brother of Chuck Norris's Walker, Texas Ranger sidekick, moves into a dorm room directly above the German house mother, blasts Public Enemy down a hallway that surprises everyone who looks inside, and settles in with a Nintendo, a coffee maker that only makes Top Ramen, and Blue Bell down the road. Deep in the heart of Texas.

Seattle Stories · TB1A · September 2001 to Now

Deadly Duck: Anger

In the dead-quiet autumn after September 11th, a laid-off Westin bell captain with a bicycle and too much time rode north out of Belltown to pick up a rubber duck. It was TB1A — one of the seven devil ducks that started the entire trackable system, and, it turns out, the twenty-sixth trackable object ever activated on Earth. What followed: six confessions that produced no real sins, an Amsterdam trip financed by unemployment, a duck that crossed the Atlantic and came back, a federal seizure by the National Park Service, a Dalmatian in Melbourne, and a Christmas Day burglary. Twenty-five years later, standing in the same Vondelpark, he went looking for the other six — and found out what his duck had actually done.

Daff Dome

The summer he was twelve, a broken-down Datsun decided the rest of Brooks's childhood. Over Sonora Pass to Mono Lake with his rock-hound mother and his friend Zack, then the car dies in Tuolumne Meadows and strands them for a week beneath Daff Dome — a Smurf city built and destroyed, a ropeless climb kept secret, both halves of a divided family converging on the same mountain road, and a yellow wire that brought the car, and the boy, home to the foothills for good. The centerpiece of the series.
